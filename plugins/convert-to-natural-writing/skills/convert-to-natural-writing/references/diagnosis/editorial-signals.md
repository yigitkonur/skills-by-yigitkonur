# Editorial Signals

Use this reference to diagnose clusters of reader-facing problems that often make prose feel generic, robotic, templated, or under-edited.

## Diagnose the mechanism

Do not count surface features. Find the mechanism that makes reading harder, less trustworthy, or less useful. A signal becomes actionable when it has:

1. an exact excerpt;
2. a contextual pattern, usually repeated or structurally important;
3. a reader-facing consequence;
4. a repair that preserves meaning and genre.

## Substance signals

| Signal | What to inspect | Reader harm | Better move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vague actor | “It is believed,” “businesses can,” “experts say” | Hides responsibility or source | Name the actor or preserve uncertainty explicitly. |
| Unsupported significance | “This marks a major shift” | Asks the reader to accept importance without evidence | State what changed and for whom. |
| Generic benefit | “improves efficiency and drives growth” | Gives no mechanism, boundary, or decision value | Name the action, metric, or practical consequence supported by sources. |
| Abstract stack | Several nominalizations in sequence | Obscures who does what | Restore actors and verbs. |
| False comprehensiveness | “from X to Y and everything in between” | Claims breadth the content does not deliver | Name the actual covered scope. |
| Repeated conclusion | Each section restates the thesis | Slows progress and makes analysis feel shallow | Give each section a distinct job. |
| Unresolved evidence | Strong claim with no source or attribution | Weakens trust | Qualify, cite, or block the claim. |

### Example: vague significance

Before:

> This innovative approach represents a significant shift in how modern teams unlock value.

Diagnosis:

- “innovative,” “significant,” and “unlock value” evaluate the approach without showing the change.
- The actor and operational consequence are missing.

After, when supported:

> The new workflow moves review before deployment, so teams catch schema mismatches without rolling back a live release.

The revision is stronger because it names mechanism and consequence, not because it avoids particular words.

## Structure signals

| Signal | Test | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formulaic opening | Could the first paragraph introduce almost any topic? | Lead with the reader's situation, finding, or decision. |
| Symmetrical section template | Do all sections repeat definition → benefits → future? | Give each section the structure its content needs. |
| Throat-clearing | Can the first two sentences disappear without loss? | Start at the first useful claim. |
| Empty summary | Does the ending merely announce that the topic matters? | End with the implication, limit, decision, or next action. |
| Transition scaffolding | Are transitions substituting for logical relationships? | Name contrast, cause, evidence, or sequence directly. |
| Fragmented lists | Would a sentence express the relationship better? | Use lists for parallel items, not as default formatting. |

## Voice signals

| Signal | Contextual question | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic authority | Does the narrator sound certain without accountable evidence? | Attribute, qualify, or state the evidence. |
| Forced intimacy | Is “you” used when the reader has no direct action? | Use the real actor or a neutral construction. |
| Corporate distance | Does passive or abstract language hide a simple action? | Name who acts and what changes. |
| Performative enthusiasm | Do intensifiers exceed the evidence or reader mood? | Let specific benefit carry the tone. |
| Voice oscillation | Does the text jump between academic, sales, and chatty registers? | Choose a register per audience and channel. |
| Unowned first person | Is “I” or “we” attached to no verified speaker? | Remove it or establish the speaker. |

## Rhythm signals

Rhythm is evidence only in context. Look for sustained patterns:

- five or more sentences with the same length and clause shape;
- repeated “not only X, but also Y” or “whether X or Y” frames;
- a series of three-item lists where the categories are not genuinely parallel;
- repeated paragraph endings that announce importance;
- excessive parenthetical nesting that interrupts the main claim;
- sentence fragments used without a purposeful voice or emphasis.

Repair rhythm after substance. Mechanical variation can hide unresolved vagueness without improving the text.

## Vocabulary clusters

Do not ban words. Investigate clusters such as:

- abstract evaluation plus no evidence: “pivotal,” “transformative,” “robust,” “seamless”;
- inflated scope: “landscape,” “realm,” “ecosystem,” when a narrower noun exists;
- generic action: “leverage,” “unlock,” “elevate,” when the mechanism is missing;
- essay scaffolding: repeated “moreover,” “furthermore,” “in conclusion” without real logical work;
- meta-description: “this section explores,” “it is important to note,” when the content can state the point.

Any one of these may be exactly right. Change it only when the surrounding sentence is vague, redundant, inflated, or misregistered.

## Multilingual signals

In localized or non-English writing, inspect for:

- source-language word order copied into the target language;
- overuse of explicit pronouns where the target language normally drops them;
- punctuation or capitalization imported from another locale;
- false friends and technically correct but locally uncommon terms;
- honorifics, formality, or second-person forms inconsistent with the relationship;
- translated idioms, humor, or metaphors that require cultural context;
- headings shaped like English noun phrases when the locale favors another form.

Do not label a dialect feature, learner feature, or regional convention as “AI.” Identify the locale mismatch and the confidence of the diagnosis.

## Diagnostic output

Use this compact form:

```text
Excerpt: “This groundbreaking solution unlocks unparalleled value.”
Issue: The sentence evaluates the solution three times but names no mechanism or outcome.
Reader impact: A technical buyer cannot tell what changes or verify the claim.
Evidence level: Contextual editorial signal; not an authorship indicator.
Smallest useful change: Replace it with the supported action and measurable or practical consequence.
```

## Common failures

| Failure | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Listing 30 “AI tells” without priorities | Report the two or three mechanisms with the largest reader impact. |
| Treating every polished sentence as suspicious | Judge purpose, evidence, and genre. |
| Fixing rhythm before claims | Repair substance and attribution first. |
| Removing all promotional language from marketing | Keep proportionate, supported persuasion tied to reader value. |
| Calling translation interference “robotic” | Name the source-target mismatch and seek fluent review when needed. |

## Completion check

- Each finding includes excerpt, mechanism, reader impact, and smallest repair.
- Signals are clustered and contextual, never word counts alone.
- Substance, structure, voice, rhythm, and locale are distinguished.
- The diagnosis remains valid if the author turns out to be human.
- The highest-impact problems are addressed before surface variety.
