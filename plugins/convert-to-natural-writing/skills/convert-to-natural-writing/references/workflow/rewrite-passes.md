# Rewrite Passes

Use this reference to rewrite in an order that improves substance before voice and surface, with regression checks after every pass.

## The sequence

Run three passes:

1. Substance and clarity
2. Voice, audience, and locale
3. Rhythm and surface

Do not blend them into one uncontrolled paraphrase. If a later pass changes a claim, return to substance and reconcile it against the ledger.

## Pass 1: substance and clarity

### Objective

Make the reasoning and action explicit without changing the evidence.

### Actions

- Put the reader's question, finding, or required action near the start.
- Name actors and verbs where abstractions hide responsibility.
- Replace unsupported evaluation with supported mechanism or consequence.
- Remove duplicate claims and empty scene-setting.
- Separate evidence, inference, recommendation, and limitation.
- Keep stable domain terminology for each concept.
- Preserve useful nuance and expert qualification.
- Mark evidence gaps instead of filling them imaginatively.

### Example

Before:

> In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, organizations are increasingly looking to leverage advanced solutions to unlock new levels of efficiency.

If the source supports only workflow automation:

> Teams are automating the handoff between review and publishing so editors no longer copy approved changes between systems.

The revision is concrete because it uses supported mechanism. Without that source detail, prefer a modest edit rather than inventing it.

### Pass-1 regression check

- Every factual proposition still appears with the same value and scope.
- No new example, benefit, metric, actor, or mechanism was invented.
- Attribution and uncertainty remain visible.
- Removed material was truly redundant or unsupported.

## Pass 2: voice, audience, and locale

### Objective

Make the clarified content sound appropriate for this reader, channel, speaker, and language variety.

### Actions

- Follow an approved voice sample or guide before the default voice.
- Choose direct or indirect address based on a real reader relationship.
- Use contractions only where natural in the locale and genre.
- Adjust explanation density to the reader's knowledge.
- Prefer concrete help over performative enthusiasm.
- Use first person only for an accountable speaker.
- Recompose syntax, politeness, terminology, and punctuation for the locale.
- Preserve identity labels and self-description where relevant.

### Example

Formal but distant:

> Users are advised to ensure that all required fields have been completed prior to submission.

Warm casual-professional, when direct instruction fits:

> Complete the required fields before you submit the form.

High-stakes policy may appropriately keep a more formal construction. The contract decides.

### Pass-2 regression check

- Warmth did not create promises, feelings, or intimacy unsupported by the speaker.
- Directness did not strengthen a recommendation into a requirement.
- Locale changes did not alter terminology, legal force, or factual scope.
- The result does not imitate translated US English by default.

## Pass 3: rhythm and surface

### Objective

Make the prose easy to follow at sentence and paragraph level without applying mechanical variation quotas.

### Actions

- Vary sentence shape where the idea benefits from a different cadence.
- Join fragments that break a relationship; split sentences that overload it.
- Remove transitions that merely announce continuation.
- Keep transitions that name a real logical relationship.
- Move modifiers next to what they qualify.
- Repair accidental repetition while preserving deliberate terminology.
- Keep lists for genuinely parallel or scannable items.
- Stop at the last useful sentence; remove empty restatements.

### Example

Before:

> The setup is simple. The setup is fast. The setup is flexible. This makes the setup a useful option for teams.

After, if all claims are supported:

> Teams can set it up quickly and adapt the same workflow as their requirements change.

Do not add “in minutes,” “without training,” or another specific promise unless supplied.

### Pass-3 regression check

- Punctuation changes did not alter code, quotations, or structured syntax.
- Sentence combining did not merge unrelated scopes or sources.
- Pronouns still have unambiguous referents.
- Deliberate repetition and stable terminology remain where useful.

## Paragraph-level review

Give every paragraph one primary job:

| Job | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Orient | Does it state the reader's situation or the section's point? |
| Explain | Does it make a mechanism or concept clear? |
| Evidence | Does it support a claim with attributed facts? |
| Qualify | Does it state limits, exceptions, or uncertainty? |
| Decide | Does it compare options or recommend an action? |
| Act | Does it tell the reader what to do next? |

Paragraphs may combine closely related jobs, but a paragraph that cycles through all six probably needs restructuring.

## Multilingual pass strategy

For non-English or mixed-language content:

1. Resolve source meaning and evidence before localization.
2. Identify locale, register, terminology, and speaker-reader relationship.
3. Recompose full clauses or paragraphs rather than swapping words.
4. Keep protected names, code, URLs, and approved terms exact.
5. Read aloud or apply language-native cadence checks when competent.
6. Require fluent review for high-stakes or low-confidence publication.

Back-translation may expose gross meaning drift, but it cannot certify naturalness or cultural fit.

## When to stop editing

Stop when:

- the reader can identify the point, evidence, limit, and next action;
- the voice fits the channel without drawing attention to itself;
- further edits would become taste-only synonym changes;
- the ledger reconciles;
- the format parses and renders;
- remaining uncertainty is explicitly owned.

More variation is not always better. Consistency is valuable for terms, UI labels, instructions, and safety language.

## Common failures

| Failure | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| One-pass paraphrasing | Separate substance, voice/locale, and surface. |
| Making prose casual before resolving claims | Complete Pass 1 first. |
| Applying sentence-length quotas | Vary only when meaning and cadence improve. |
| Replacing stable terms with synonyms | Keep one term per concept. |
| Using back-translation as native review | Treat it as a drift probe, then use a fluent reviewer. |

## Completion check

- Pass 1 made meaning concrete without invented detail.
- Pass 2 matched reader, speaker, genre, and locale.
- Pass 3 improved cadence without detector-facing pattern changes.
- Each pass was reconciled against exact, value, force, and document protections.
- Editing stopped before useful consistency became artificial variety.
